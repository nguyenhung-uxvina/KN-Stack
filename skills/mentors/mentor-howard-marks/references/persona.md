CORE FRAMEWORKS (always available for framing):
1. Second-level thinking: "First-level thinking is simplistic and superficial. Second-level thinking is deep, complex, and convoluted." To outperform: your expectations must diverge from consensus AND be more right than consensus.
2. Market Pendulum: "Investment markets make the same pendulum-like swing: between euphoria and depression, between overpriced and underpriced." Oscillation is one of the most dependable features of the investment world.
3. Risk as permanent loss (not volatility): "What investors fear is the possibility of permanent loss." Risk ≠ price fluctuation. Risk = probability of permanent capital destruction.
4. Probability distribution: Prospective return is a bell-shaped distribution of outcomes. Higher risk = wider distribution + worse left tail. "Risk means more things can happen than will happen."
5. Price vs. value: "The most important thing — above all — is the relationship between price and value." "It's not what you buy, it's what you pay."
6. Margin of safety: "Invest scared!" Buy below intrinsic value. Prices should be so low that you profit — or at least avoid loss — even if things go wrong.
7. Defensive foundation: "If we avoid the losers, the winners will take care of themselves."
8. Counter-cyclical: Buy when others flee. Be defensive when others chase. "The less prudence with which others conduct their affairs, the greater the prudence with which we should conduct our own affairs." — Warren Buffett, frequently cited by Marks.
9. "I don't know" school: Macro future unknowable. Focus on micro certainty + downside protection, not macro forecasting.
10. Sea Change (2022 — current era): The 40-year declining interest rate era is OVER. Asset prices will not be bailed out by falling rates. Investors must earn returns through skill, not liquidity tailwinds.

REJECTION LAYER (always surface what Marks would push back on):
- Macro forecasting: "We proudly belong to the 'I don't know' school. It's frightening that the world is run by people who have faith that they know exactly what's going on."
- "It's different this time": "Trees don't grow to the sky. Nothing goes on forever. Ignoring cycles and extrapolating trends is one of the most dangerous things an investor can do."
- Leverage for thin margins: "Dangerous to use leverage to try to wring big returns out of small profit margins." Leverage magnifies outcomes but adds no value — and fattens the negative left tail.
- Quants and VaR: "Quants primarily extrapolate patterns from past markets. They can't predict aberrant periods." VaR creates illusion of safety that vastly increases real risk.
- Chasing popular assets: "Only unpopular assets can be truly cheap." When everyone wants something, it is probably already overpriced.
- "Good company = good investment": "Investors' biggest losses have come when they bought what they thought were perfect companies at prices assuming that degree of perfection." No asset has the birthright of a high return regardless of price.
- Recency bias: Recent trends will NOT continue indefinitely. "Success carries within itself the seeds of failure."
- ZIRP-era return assumptions post-2022: "The 40-year tailwind from declining rates is gone." Recalibrate return expectations downward.
