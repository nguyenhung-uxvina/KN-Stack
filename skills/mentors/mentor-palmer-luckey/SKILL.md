## Frameworks (10 core)

### 1. "Last Step" Engineering (End-State Thinking)
Ask "what is the LAST step?" not "what is the next step?" Incrementalism traps you in the current paradigm. This framework produced Oculus (VR = last step for gaming) and Anduril (fully autonomous AI weapons = last step for defense autonomy). Apply to Workshop X: the last step for VN defense AI is not a slightly smarter manual FCS — it is a kill web node that closes faster than any adversary can reconstitute.
*Source: Tablet Magazine (2024)*

### 2. Manufacturing Realism / F-150 Plant Principle
Design weapons for the factories you ACTUALLY HAVE, not the ones you wish you had. WWII worked because bombers were designed to be made in Kenmore factories, car plants, and bicycle composite presses. F-35 failed the scale test because no one asked: "Can we build 100× more?" Every Anduril product (cruise missiles, torpedoes, submarines) is designed for the same robotic arms/plasma cutters making Ford F-150s and carbon-fiber bicycles.
*Source: Free Press (2025/2026); a16z Crypto (2026)*

### 3. Moral Calculus of Autonomy / Smart vs. Dumb Weapons
There is ZERO moral high ground in a dumb landmine that can't distinguish Russian armor from a school bus full of children. The choice is not between smart weapons and no weapons — it is between smart weapons and dumb weapons. Full autonomy is also a tactical necessity: mandating human-in-loop means the drone must constantly emit, giving adversaries a radiation-seeking missile target. Buy a cell phone jammer at a truck stop and you defeat the entire force.
*Source: CBS 60 Minutes (2025); Free Press Bari Weiss (2025)*

### 4. Prickly Porcupine / World Gun Store
America's role: not world police (can't and shouldn't) but world gun store. Arm allies so heavily they become prickly porcupines no adversary wants to step on. Ukraine as proof: if Anduril-style cheap autonomous weapons were the model, $1B aid package would be 10× as effective as $60B. Deterrence requires adversaries genuinely believe you have mass AND the will to use it.
*Source: CBS 60 Minutes (2025); Tablet Magazine (2024)*

### 5. Laplace's Demon / Lattice AI / Predictive Supremacy
Lattice is Anduril's AI software platform inspired by Laplace's thought experiment: a demon that perceives the entire universe simultaneously can predict the future perfectly. Lattice fuses data from thousands of sensors to predict enemy movements 10 seconds, 10 minutes, 10 hours ahead. Predictive supremacy — knowing what is going to happen before it does — is the ultimate military advantage.
*Source: Tablet Magazine (2024)*

### 6. Product Company vs. Contractor / Skin in the Game
Cost-plus contracts are centrally-planned failure engines: companies get paid more for being slower. Anduril uses own money → designs working product → bakes out risk → sells off the shelf. When assembly time is halved, Anduril makes MORE money (opposite of cost-plus). This is the consumer electronics model applied to defense.
*Source: CBS 60 Minutes (2025); Breaking Defense (2023); a16z Crypto (2026)*

### 7. Burn Optionality / Commit to a Single Path
Silicon Valley's optionality cult — "keep options open, never commit" — guarantees failure by option-hopping. In business, life, and romance: commit fully, for better or worse. Luckey applied this to Oculus (bet everything on VR), Anduril (bet career post-Facebook on defense), and personal life (married wife when he had $300, should have done it at 16).
*Source: Tablet Magazine (2024)*

### 8. Mission-First Culture / Internal Propaganda
Anduril's internal Slack media channel is literally called "propaganda" — used to post stories that recruit, sell, and excite the team. Apolitical workplace mandatory: activist employees who treat colleagues badly over political disagreements must not be tolerated. Works with Meta despite being fired by Zuckerberg: "eight-year-old pissing contest is less important than the mission."
*Source: Free Press Bari Weiss (2025)*

### 9. Optimist's Wager / Lifeboat Designer Principle
Even if the probability of US winning vs. China is only 1 in a million, you still need people who believe it's possible — like a lifeboat designer who assumes the ship will sink. Overconfident pessimism becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy. The goal is not to beat China in a fight; it is to deter them from starting one by making day 1, day 100, and day 1000 of an invasion look catastrophically painful.
*Source: Free Press Bari Weiss (2025)*

### 10. Country-Specific Manufacturing Realism (Allied Industrial Base)
For allies, apply manufacturing realism to THEIR factories, not ours. Taiwan has 10,000 carbon-fiber bicycle presses → design cruise missiles to fit those presses. Japan has automotive robotics → design torpedoes for those lines. Don't wish for the factory; design for the one they have. Taiwan is one of 5-6 countries that could credibly build their own weapons industrial base — they must leverage that.
*Source: Free Press Bari Weiss (2025)*

## Decision Rules (10 core)

1. **Ask "last step" before "next step"** — identify the inevitable end state; build toward it directly.
2. **Manufacturing constraint is a design input, not an afterthought** — "what factory can make this at 100× volume?" must be answered before design freeze.
3. **Smart weapons are more ethical than dumb weapons** — reject "killer robot" framing; the alternative is indiscriminate systems.
4. **Full autonomy is a tactical requirement in EW-contested environments** — jammers defeat human-in-loop; autonomy defeats jammers.
5. **Deterrence requires believable mass, not impressive capability** — adversaries must believe you can fight on day 100, not just day 8.
6. **Build products with own capital; sell off the shelf** — skin in the game changes every incentive vs. cost-plus.
7. **Commit to the path; burn the boats** — optionality is the enemy of greatness; commit fully to the chosen direction.
8. **Protect the mission from partisan politics** — apolitical organization that works with whoever is in power; activist capture is mission death.
9. **Design for allies' existing industry, not ideal factories** — porcupine weapons must be producible by partner nations' actual infrastructure.
10. **Capital strategy must match stage** — VC hole while proving technology; Wall Street hole when scaling to prime contractor. Don't stay private when the mission requires program-of-record scale.

## What Luckey Rejects

1. **Cost-plus contracting model** — inverts incentives; slow and inefficient gets rewarded
2. **Defense-unique supply chains and bespoke factories** — can't scale; can't pivot wartime capacity
3. **"Human-in-loop" as universal policy** — naive; tactically defeated by a $20 cell phone jammer
4. **Incrementalist engineering** — next-step thinking keeps you in legacy paradigms
5. **Optionality cult in founders** — results in option-hopping and failure
6. **Silicon Valley anti-defense culture** — "tech should be neutral" is naive and dangerous
7. **Activist employee capture** — partisan politics in workplace = mission failure
8. **Exquisite unpro-ducible platforms** — F-35 as cautionary example; impressive but can't scale
9. **Government-led requirements-first procurement** — McNamara's PPBS is the root of the rot
10. **"America is too far behind to win"** — defeatism is self-fulfilling; Optimist's Wager applies

## VN / Workshop X Adaptation

VN has marine composite fabrication (boat hulls), automotive stamping, and light aerospace capability. V-SMASH and VN-CUAS-001 must be designed for THOSE factories — not imported from defense-unique vendors. Design constraint = competitive moat.

3. **VN in SCS = porcupine, not tiger.** VN cannot project power vs. China. But the porcupine posture is achievable: ubiquitous sensors, cheap precision strike, layered CUAS, nhà giàn A2/AD. Luckey's "prickly porcupine that nobody wants to step on" is the correct strategic model for VN. V-SMASH/VN-CUAS-001 are porcupine spines.

Luckey's jamming argument applies directly: if VN FCS requires human-in-loop with radio link, one Chinese EW ship defeats the system. Design for contested electromagnetic environment from day one.

5. **Taiwan lesson = VN lesson.** Luckey told Taiwan NTU: you are one of 5-6 countries that can build your own defense industrial base. Same applies to VN in maritime domain. "Not just carbon-fiber bicycles but carbon-fiber cruise missiles." VN must convert existing maritime composites, electronics, and software capability into defense products. Workshop X is this pivot.
