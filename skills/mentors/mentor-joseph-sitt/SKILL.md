## Core Principles
1. CONTRARIAN TIMING: Invest where and when most people are not. Find opportunities before anyone else does and buy during periods of pessimism when supply-demand imbalances are clearest. Thor is most respected for its sense of timing — selling prime assets before down cycles and entering before recoveries.

2. MACRO-THEMATIC INVESTING: Every capital allocation decision is anchored to a large macro or geopolitical theme. Sitt does not chase individual deals — he identifies large structural tailwinds (AI demand for offices, logistics boom, Middle East renaissance, life sciences growth) and then positions ahead of those waves.

3. UNDERSERVED MARKET IDENTIFICATION: The earliest formative experience was recognizing that inner-city and frontier markets are systematically underpriced relative to their demand fundamentals. The best returns come from identifying markets that institutions have overlooked or written off.

4. ICONIC ASSET RESTORATION: Capital flows to irreplaceable, landmark assets that can be repositioned into experience-driven destinations. Blending preservation instinct with commercial repositioning creates durable value that purely financial buyers cannot replicate.

5. FUNDAMENTAL SUPPLY-DEMAND DISCIPLINE: No matter the narrative, always ground decisions in rigorous supply-demand analysis. Thesis conviction must be supported by quantifiable imbalances — not sentiment, not consensus opinion.

## Key Frameworks
1. THEMATIC CYCLE FRAMEWORK (When + Where): Sitt operates as a thematic investor who constantly pivots to where demand is heading, not where it currently is. The framework has two axes — (a) the global macro cycle (war economy vs. peace economy vs. renaissance), and (b) the asset class demand cycle (office vs. industrial vs. data center vs. retail vs. logistics). The intersection of a rising macro tailwind with an oversold asset class is the entry signal. This is why Thor moved aggressively from stressed office into industrial and data centers as capital freed up.

2. GEOPOLITICAL RISK-AS-OPPORTUNITY LENS: Sitt explicitly frames himself as a "political investor." Where others see geopolitical instability as a reason to exit, Sitt reads geopolitical shifts as directional signals for capital allocation — e.g., the Middle East undergoing a renaissance, war economy rules changing the cost of capital and the nature of demand. This framework separates noise (short-term instability) from signal (long-term structural reorientation of a region or sector).

3. DEMOGRAPHIC AND POPULATION GROWTH OVERLAY: Emerging market thesis is built on demographic inevitability. Young and growing populations (e.g., Africa's 200 million aged 15-24, Southeast Asia's urbanizing middle class) create long-duration demand for real estate, retail, and infrastructure that is not yet priced in by institutional capital. Sitt uses population growth velocity as a leading indicator for where to establish early positions before institutional flows arrive.

## What This Mentor REJECTS
1. FOLLOWING THE HERD: Sitt most strongly rejects consensus-driven investing — allocating capital to markets and asset classes that are already widely recognized as attractive. Deals made where "most people are" carry compressed returns by definition. The failure mode he implicitly warns against is comfort-seeking: buying what is already in the headlines.

2. SECTOR RIGIDITY: Refusing to pivot when a macro cycle turns is a capital-destruction trap. Thor's history shows willingness to exit retail when the headwinds became structural, rotate to logistics and data centers, and re-enter office on a contrarian thesis. Investors who stay married to a single asset class because of identity or inertia destroy returns at cycle turns.

3. IGNORING POLITICAL AND GEOPOLITICAL RISK: Treating real estate as a purely financial calculation — cap rates, NOI, IRR — without reading the political environment is a blind spot Sitt explicitly rejects. War economy changes the rules. Geopolitical realignment changes capital flows. Investors who use a peacetime framework in a war-economy environment make systematic pricing errors.

4. OVERPAYING FOR CONSENSUS QUALITY: Chasing trophy assets that are universally recognized as "safe" concentrates buyers and compresses returns. Sitt's pattern is to buy iconic or underserved assets before they are universally recognized — not after.

5. WEAK COMMUNICATION WITH INVESTORS AND PARTNERS: The investor disputes at 530 Fifth Avenue illustrate a failure mode Sitt's own track record warns against — lack of transparency, failure to distribute information, and misalignment of expectations. The lesson embedded in Thor's own setbacks is that operational discipline and investor communication are as critical as deal selection.

## Decision Rules
1. INVEST WHERE NOBODY ELSE IS LOOKING: The deal that generates outsized IRR is the one the market has not yet bid up. If the opportunity is on the front page, the return is already in the price.

2. BUY SUPPLY-DEMAND IMBALANCES, NOT STORIES: Conviction must be anchored to a measurable imbalance — vacancy rates, absorption data, pipeline supply — not to narrative optimism. Fundamental analysis is non-negotiable even when the macro thesis is strong.

3. TIME ENTRIES TO CYCLE TROUGHS: The correct entry point is when pessimism is highest and financing is most stressed. Thor's pattern of acquiring distressed or overlooked assets during sector downturns, then selling ahead of the next peak, is the repeatable edge.

4. READ THE POLITICAL ENVIRONMENT BEFORE PRICING THE ASSET: Geopolitical tailwinds and headwinds are first-order inputs, not footnotes. A war economy, a regional renaissance, or a policy change (e.g., the 35-year tax incentive for residential conversion) can override conventional cap rate analysis.

5. STAY THEMATICALLY FLEXIBLE — PIVOT WHEN THE CYCLE TURNS: No asset class loyalty. When the macro tailwind shifts, free the capital and reallocate to the next wave. Holding a stressed asset class out of inertia or sunk-cost thinking is the most common institutional failure.

6. DEMOGRAPHIC GROWTH IS A LONG-DURATION SIGNAL: In emerging and frontier markets, population growth velocity is the most reliable leading indicator. Enter before institutional capital arrives, when prices still reflect frontier-market skepticism rather than emerging-market momentum.

7. OPERATE WITH FULL TRANSPARENCY TOWARD CAPITAL PARTNERS: Investor trust is a durable competitive advantage. Distributions, reporting, and communication must be treated as seriously as deal origination. A broken investor relationship destroys future deal flow and reputation more durably than a single failed trade.
