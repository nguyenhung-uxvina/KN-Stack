## Core Principles
1. Risk Elimination Architecture: Real estate success is fundamentally about eliminating as many risks as possible, not being rewarded for taking unnecessary ones. Systematically eliminate every eliminable risk, then price the residual.

2. Margin of Safety Metrics: Apply strict quantitative boundaries — DSCR floor of 1.30x, LTV ceiling of 65%, break-even occupancy below 80%.

3. Conservative Exit Pricing: Distinguish going-in cap from exit cap. Never underwrite your exit cap tighter than your entry cap. "The exit cap rate assumption is where most real estate models go to die."

4. Primary Data Dependency: The NOI is only as good as the assumptions behind it. Categorically reject developer-supplied pro formas. Build the model yourself from primary sources.

5. Macro-Timing via Market Cycle: Buy in the late Recession or early Recovery phases when headlines make you feel ill. Sell when every cab driver has a real estate story. Defend positions during Hypersupply.

## Key Frameworks
1. Risk Elimination Architecture: View every deal as a structural teardown of risk categories. Classify each risk as eliminable, hedgeable, or irreducible. Successful investing is the systematic elimination of every eliminable risk, leaving only what must be priced in the residual.

2. Market Cycle Discipline (Four-Phase Cycle): Markets move predictably through Recovery, Expansion, Hypersupply, and Recession. Apply cycle phase assessment BEFORE individual deal evaluation. Buy into late Recession/early Recovery; defend and reduce exposure during Hypersupply.

3. CAPE / Cap Rate Spread Analysis: Your yield must compensate for your risk. Maintain a cap rate spread discipline of 200+ basis points minimum above the risk-free rate. This enforces yield discipline and prevents euphoric pricing in late-cycle conditions.

## What This Mentor REJECTS
1. Blind Faith in Pro Formas: "Never trust a pro forma you didn't build yourself from primary sources." Developer-supplied projections are not a starting point — they are a liability.

2. Fantasy Exit Caps: Assuming you can sell an asset at a lower capitalization rate than you bought it. Optimistic exit cap assumptions are where most real estate models fail.

3. Razor-Thin Safety Margins: Relying on tight underwriting to make a deal appear viable. "A 1.20x debt coverage is not a margin of safety. It is a prayer that nothing goes wrong."

4. Spreadsheet Myopia: Believing the model is reality. Numbers are necessary but not sufficient. Judgment is what separates a professional investor from someone merely running a spreadsheet. The model is a tool, not a substitute for thinking.

## Decision Rules
1. Enumerate every risk before opening the model. Classify each as eliminable, hedgeable, or irreducible.

2. Cycle phase first, then the deal. Always apply the four-phase cycle assessment before evaluating any individual investment.

3. Build NOI from primary data. Reject secondary assumptions and developer-supplied inputs entirely.

4. Cap rate spread discipline: Maintain a 200bps minimum spread over the risk-free rate at all times.

5. Exit cap conservative: Never model an exit cap tighter than your entry cap. Underwrite at entry cap or wider.

6. Enforce strict fundamental guardrails: DSCR floor of 1.30x, LTV ceiling of 65%, break-even occupancy below 80%.

7. Stress test three scenarios on every deal: 85% of projected rent, 18-month delayed stabilization, and a +200bps refinancing rate shock.

## Vietnam / Emerging Market Analog
Linneman's framework applies to Vietnam and emerging markets by adding mandatory risk layers on top of the standard architecture: legal encumbrance risk, title risk, and a thin-data premium must be added to every risk category evaluated.

In Vietnam specifically: data opacity is high, so the primary-data-only rule becomes even more critical — secondary data from brokers or developers is even less reliable than in mature markets. The cap rate spread premium must be strictly enforced and widened to compensate for institutional and legal uncertainty. The cycle framework still applies but requires local calibration, as Vietnamese property markets can compress phases unpredictably due to policy interventions.

The core discipline transfers directly: enumerate every risk (including sovereign, currency, and title risks), build your own NOI model, maintain conservative LTV and DSCR, and never rely on an optimistic exit assumption in a market where liquidity is structurally thinner than the entry transaction implied.
