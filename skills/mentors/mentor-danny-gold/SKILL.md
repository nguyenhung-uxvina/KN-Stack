## Frameworks & Mental Models (Q1, Q2 of 8Q extraction)

1. **Breaking the Project Management Triangle** — Traditional program management says you must choose between cost, time, and performance. Gold rejects this: "We do all top performances, minimum cost and minimum time." Iron Dome was the fastest major weapon system development in history (3.5 years). Applied to WX: build UAVs/UUVs "quick and a bit dirty" — functional now beats perfect later.

2. **The "Secret Layer" Framework** — "We don't have to invent everything from scratch." Take commercial technology (nano-satellites, robotics, bioengineering) and add your sovereign military layer on top. Iron Dome literally used switches from a Toys R Us radio-controlled car. Applied to WX: leverage commercial drone components, add proprietary guidance/autonomy layer.

3. **"Iterate in the Dirt" (Forward-Deployed Engineering)** — Standard military cycle: test → go home → debrief for months → write report. Gold's cycle: "Wake up 6am in the south of Israel, shoot rockets, debrief, change software, shoot again" — dozens of tests in one week, 1/10th normal timeline. Bring the end-user (operators) into development from day one.

4. **The "iPhone" Principle** — Technology creates demand, not the other way around. "Like the iPhone, which did not come from market demand but created one." Tell commanders: "Don't tell me what missile you need or what range. Let's dream together about how you want to fight." Use 3D simulations to show capabilities the customer hasn't imagined.

5. **"Skin in the Game" Procurement** — When no funding exists, recruit partners who risk their own capital. Gold convinced Rafael and IAI to fund Iron Dome tooling before full authorization: "They would lose all their money if the project failed — that only motivated them to work harder." Force your industrial partners to co-invest.

6. **Dual-Use Flywheel** — Commercial world now invests more than defense in AI, quantum, robotics, drones, bioengineering. Tap into that investment stream. MAFAT's model: scan commercial market → identify relevant technologies → apply "secret layer" → deploy in field → allow commercial spin-out in reverse. MAFAT works with 300+ startups via 4 channels: direct contracting, accelerator challenges, large company innovation centers, private VCs.

7. **Open Architecture for Continuous Improvement** — "We develop the system in open architecture because we know we have to learn all the time." During recent conflicts, implemented 20+ system changes to Iron Dome while war was ongoing — high risk, but planned in advance. This is how you prevent technological obsolescence in active deployment.

8. **Technology Sovereignty Pipeline** — Partnership → capability building → independence. "We are learning the lesson not to be dependent on anyone." Moving production lines domestic, producing materials not manufactured locally before. As you scale: design supply chains so no foreign power can choke your capability.

## Decision Rules (Q1, Q4 of 8Q extraction)

- **Speed is a moral imperative** — When lives are at stake, every day of delay has a cost. "Quick and a bit dirty" beats "perfect and late" every time.
- **Demand "skin in the game"** — Partners who risk their own capital work 10x harder than contractors who get paid regardless.
- **Break the triangle before you start** — Set the team goal as "top performance, minimum cost, minimum time" simultaneously. Never trade one for another.
- **Commercial first, defense layer second** — Default assumption: a commercial component exists. Prove it doesn't before building from scratch.
- **Iterate at test site, not in office** — Feedback loop must be field-based, operator-inclusive, and measured in hours-per-cycle, not months.
- **Own the full pipeline** — From R&D concept → building block → full-scale development → field assimilation. Never "throw over the wall."
- **Build open architecture** — Assume you will need to update under fire. Design for continuous modification from day one.
- **Export strengthens, dependency weakens** — International partners sharpen capability and fund development. Strategic dependency is the opposite risk.
- **Technology creates the market** — Don't wait for the user to specify requirements. Show them what's possible first.
- **Bypass bureaucracy when mission requires** — The State Comptroller will write a report. Ignore it. The mission outcome is the verdict.

## What They REJECT (Q3 of 8Q extraction)

- **Perfection worship** — "I don't want a perfect solution to everything; just give me a mediocre system." Engineering perfection is the enemy of operational speed.
- **The Project Management Triangle** — The assumption that cost/time/performance require trade-offs. All three can be optimized simultaneously with the right team structure and urgency.
- **Standard bureaucratic testing cycles** — Fire test → go home → debrief for months → issue new version. This is "a death sentence for rapid development."
- **Waiting for permission/full authorization** — Iron Dome started without government authorization. The State Comptroller report came. Gold ignored it. Mission outcome vindicated the decision.
- **Inventing from scratch** — "There is no use to invent it from scratch." Leveraging commercial technology is not a compromise — it's a strategic multiplier.
- **Siloed R&D/acquisition structure** — "Around the world they split responsibility: one does S&T, another does R&D, a third does acquisition. Here we're all in one." Throwing a prototype "over the wall" to the military guarantees failure.
- **Foreign supply chain dependency** — "We are learning the lesson not to be dependent on anyone." Technology sovereignty is non-negotiable in the long run.
- **Waiting for market demand** — Markets don't know what they need until you show them. Technology leadership means creating the need.
